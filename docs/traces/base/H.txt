FINAL: The papers *Chain-of-Thought Prompting* (CoT) and *ReAct* treat intermediate reasoning in distinct ways, reflecting their different goals:

*   **Chain-of-Thought (CoT):** This approach treats intermediate reasoning as a **static, internal cognitive process**. By providing examples of step-by-step reasoning in few-shot prompts, the model learns to decompose complex problems into a sequence of logical steps before arriving at a final answer. Because this reasoning happens entirely within the model's latent space, it is limited by the model's internal knowledge and is prone to hallucination on tasks requiring external information.

*   **ReAct:** This approach treats intermediate reasoning as a **dynamic, interactive component of a decision loop**. ReAct interleaves "thought" steps with "action" steps (such as tool use or web searches). Unlike CoT, where reasoning is purely internal, ReAct uses reasoning to plan and adjust actions, and then uses the results of those actions to update its subsequent reasoning. This allows the model to ground its reasoning in external facts, mitigating the drift and hallucination issues inherent in purely internal CoT.

In summary, while CoT focuses on eliciting a structured internal thought process to improve reasoning performance, ReAct extends this by integrating reasoning with external agency to bridge the gap between internal logic and real-world information.
