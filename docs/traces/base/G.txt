FINAL: The credit assignment problem (CAP) in reinforcement learning refers to the challenge of determining which specific actions, among a sequence of many, are responsible for a delayed outcome or reward. Because agents often navigate complex environments where rewards are sparse or distant, traditional algorithms struggle to distribute "credit" or "blame" accurately across the trajectory of decisions.

Based on the provided literature, researchers approach this problem through several distinct methodologies:

*   **Hindsight Credit Assignment:** Rather than relying solely on "foresight" (predicting future outcomes from the present), some approaches utilize hindsight information. By looking back at the trajectory after the outcome is known, agents can more effectively associate past actions with the resulting success or failure.
*   **Inference of Immediate Rewards:** To address the temporal delay between actions and rewards, some algorithms use neural networks to infer immediate, intermediate rewards from the final delayed signal. This helps the agent understand the value of individual steps within a long sequence, such as in Intelligent Tutoring Systems where a student's final learning gain is the only clear metric.
*   **Retrospective vs. Prospective Perspectives:** Some frameworks distinguish between "influence functions" (which operate in forethought to predict the future) and "assignment" (which operates in retrospection to update past states). By shifting from a purely forward-looking perspective to a backward-looking one, agents can better map outcomes back to the specific decisions that influenced them.
*   **Bayesian Modeling and Forgetting:** In biological and human-centric studies, credit assignment is modeled using Bayesian frameworks. These models often incorporate "forgetting" mechanisms, which allow the agent to prioritize recent or more relevant action-reward pairs, helping them solve the problem even when the target movement or goal is not explicitly defined.
*   **Structural and Algorithmic Improvements:** Other approaches attempt to move beyond simple global reward signals (which suffer from high variance as the number of units or actions increases). These include using reward shaping, options discovery, and leveraging prior knowledge (such as from Large Language Models) to guide the agent's value function, thereby making the credit assignment process more efficient and scalable.
