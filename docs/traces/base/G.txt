### Summary of Approaches

*   **Attention Is All You Need (Attention):** Addresses credit assignment by replacing sequential processing with a global **self-attention mechanism**. By allowing every token to attend to every other token in the sequence simultaneously, the model can directly assign "credit" (weight) to relevant distant tokens regardless of their position, overcoming the limitations of recurrent models that struggle with long-range dependencies.
*   **Chain-of-Thought (CoT):** Shifts credit assignment from a purely internal hidden-state process to an **explicit, step-by-step reasoning process**. By forcing the model to generate intermediate reasoning steps, it decomposes complex problems into smaller, manageable parts. This allows the model to assign credit to specific logical steps that lead to the final answer, rather than relying on a single "black box" inference.
*   **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO):** Tackles credit assignment in the context of **alignment**. Unlike traditional RLHF, which requires training a separate reward model to assign credit to generated sequences, DPO mathematically reformulates the objective to optimize the policy directly on preference data. It implicitly assigns credit by increasing the probability of preferred responses and decreasing the probability of dispreferred ones without needing an explicit reward function.
*   **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation):** Focuses on **parameter-efficient credit assignment** during fine-tuning. Instead of updating all weights (which distributes credit across the entire parameter space), LoRA freezes the pre-trained weights and injects trainable low-rank matrices. Credit assignment is constrained to these smaller, low-rank updates, which effectively capture the necessary task-specific adaptations without modifying the original model's core knowledge.
*   **ReAct:** Combines reasoning and acting to solve the credit assignment problem in **interactive environments**. By interleaving "thought" traces with "action" steps, the model can assign credit to specific external observations or tool outputs. This allows the model to adjust its strategy based on feedback from the environment, effectively linking actions to their consequences in a way that pure reasoning or pure acting cannot.

### Comparison

| Paper | Primary Focus | Credit Assignment Mechanism |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Attention** | Architecture | Global connectivity via self-attention weights. |
| **CoT** | Reasoning | Decomposition into explicit, sequential logical steps. |
| **DPO** | Alignment | Direct optimization of policy based on preference pairs. |
| **LoRA** | Efficiency | Constraining updates to low-rank parameter matrices. |
| **ReAct** | Interaction | Linking internal reasoning to external action feedback. |

In summary, while **Attention** and **LoRA** modify the structural way models process or update information, **CoT** and **ReAct** modify the *process* of generation to make credit assignment more transparent or grounded. **DPO** stands apart by simplifying the *objective* of credit assignment, removing the need for a separate reward model in alignment.
